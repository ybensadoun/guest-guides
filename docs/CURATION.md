# What a guide recommends (curation rule, from the Ca La Kimberly owner, 18 Oct 2026)

A guide is worth opening only if it shows what a guest would NOT find by searching Google themselves.
Apply this to every researched list (things to do, places to eat, markets, events) and to anything an agent or the onboarding suggests.

## Include
- Hidden or easy-to-miss things nearby that a guest would not think to search for: a golf practice range 10 minutes away for a quick session, a natural spring on the beach, a dog beach, the local weekly markets.
- Places the host loves and vouches for (their restaurants, their bakery), with their own words.
- Things picked for a kind of guest: "Great for kids" places kids will really love, dog-friendly spots.
- Practical things that are hard to find in the moment (late pizza delivery, recycling rules, the nearest pharmacy on duty).

## Leave out
- What any guest finds in one search and does not need us for: supermarkets and groceries, petrol stations, generic chains, the obvious big sights unless the host has a real tip.
- Specialist lists the specialist already knows: a golfer searches for golf courses, so we do not list golf courses. Exception: one truly exceptional place worth highlighting, with a reason.
- Anything we cannot confirm still exists (see "Data accuracy" in PROJECTS.md: recent source and a live Google Maps listing).

## How to search (guide builder, research agents)
- Start from the house location and radius, then ask "what is close that a visitor would not know about?" rather than "what are the top places?".
- Prefer small, specific, local things with a clear reason to go (cheap, quick, unusual, great for kids, dog friendly, loved by the host).
- Every item needs a one-line reason in the description saying why it is worth it (for example: "close to the house and ideal for a quick practice").
- Quality over quantity: a short list of good tips beats a long list that looks like Google.
