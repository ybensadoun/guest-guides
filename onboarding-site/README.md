# Porta onboarding (public site)

Live at https://porta-guides.netlify.app (Netlify project `porta-guides`).

- `index.html` is the deployed page. Answers and photos stay in the visitor's browser (localStorage + IndexedDB) until they press Send.
- On Send it posts to Netlify Forms: `onboarding` (answers, device, walkthrough timings), one `file` submission per photo,
  `note` (the "Missing a question here?" notes, sent immediately), `feedback` (review rounds, `?feedback=<sid>&guide=<slug>`).
- Edit the sources in `src/` and rebuild: `cd src && python3 build.py` (writes `src/index.html`; copy it up one level).
- Deploy: Netlify (porta-guides) builds from GitHub (guest-guides, main, base directory onboarding-site). Test first, commit on main, the user clicks Push origin in GitHub Desktop. Anything pushed to main goes live. No dragging.

Left as written: anything inside `translate="no"` (amenity names, already in the language; the product name), what is typed in a field, and names the table lists in `_keep`. `build.py` checks the table (all three languages, the same gaps, no em-dashes) and adds the French spaces. A new sentence in English shows in English until it is in the table: `test/check.mjs` lists every sentence it saw that the table lacks in `test/shots/i18n-missing.json` (guide content and test names appear there too; only the maker's own sentences belong in the table), and `CHECK_LANG=fr node onboarding-site/test/check.mjs` runs the same screens with the maker in French (checks that look for English words fail then, as expected; the layout checks are the ones that count). The maker's language is also the guide's: Claude writes the first version in it (`/api/listing` gets `lang`; tried 7 Nov on Ca La Kimberly's listing with Haiku 4.5: Spanish clean, Catalan right for the rules, beds and rooms with a few Spanish words left in the welcome, which the build's translation puts right; Haiku 5.5, possible through `ANTHROPIC_MODEL`, writes better Catalan but left out house rules at the low effort the function's time allows), `answers.sourceLang` keeps it (set when the listing is read, else on Publish), the build translates from it, and the worker's translation sorts out texts in another language or a mix (Catalan hosts often mix Catalan and Spanish). The editor opens on the guide's own language (`answers.sourceLang`; a guide without it, like Ca La Kimberly, in English as before). Not translated yet: the emails (Supabase sign-up and password emails, the worker's report).
- Rooms and photos (1 Nov 2026, Yohann: "each room as a section in the same way as the preview, and the user can organise the ranking"): the `photos` step is one card per room (`roomCard`, `roomsNow` in `src/start.js`): its name, its photos, what guests should know there, arrows for the order. `answers.rooms` keeps them, `[{ n: name, c: kind of room, t: notes }]`; a photo belongs to a room by name (`roomLabel`). Tapping a photo opens the viewer, where it moves to another room. A room with no photo stays until the host removes it. The one order is the house page's and the house guide's, and the phone shows it at once; the editor has the same arrows on the house page's rooms and the house guide's parts. A photo with no room waits in "Not in a room yet". Without a listing the usual rooms are offered. The listing's room names reach the builder in English (`roomNames` in `/api/listing`): the photos come with the page, the English names a little later, and a room still named by its caption then takes its English name (`nameListingRooms`, 5 Nov 2026); a room the host renamed stays theirs.
- What the place offers (2 Nov 2026, Yohann: "prefill the most used amenities and add a search, as Airbnb does"; its own step after Rooms and photos): one list, `engine/src/amenities.json` (116 amenities in Airbnb's 13 groups plus "Also", each named in the six languages, with Airbnb's own codes). `answers.amenities` holds its ids, plus the host's own words for anything not in it. The step (`src/amen.js`) has a search (any language), the most asked for, every group to open and the host's own; the listing read ticks all of the listing's amenities (all of them, not only the 10 its page shows: `/api/listing` reads the "Show all" list from the page data and gives Airbnb's code for each, `amenityItems`, so no Claude and no guessing). The builder's former chips ("BBQ", "Washing machine") turn into ids by themselves, and so do a listing's own words in any of the six languages (the list's names, plus Airbnb's own titles in `k`, such as "Lavadora en el alojamiento (Gratis)"); for older answers the generator leaves out a listing word the list does not know, so nothing shows twice (3 Nov 2026). The House page lists them by group, the most asked for first (`amenTop`); the editor's House page adds one from the list into its group, in every language of the guide.
- Photos: the listing's photos (with Airbnb's own captions) are added to "Rooms and photos" with a guessed room, and the host confirms them. They are sent as links in the `listing_photos` form field; `engine/scripts/generate-site.py` downloads them into the right rooms.
- Step 1 is "Load your listing": it calls /api/listing (reads the page directly, or through the public reader r.jina.ai when Airbnb refuses servers; then falls back to pasted text), fills the next steps and adds the photos. /api/photorooms asks Claude to look at the photos and propose a room for each. "Guide sections" comes right after "About the place" and decides which question steps follow.
- "The look" (onboarding and editor, decided 28 Oct 2026): 1. logo (optional, none shown without one), 2. cover picture (one of the listing photos, an upload, drawn from the colours, or an artistic version of the chosen photo, made by the worker's `cover-art` job; the host sees it and keeps it or tries again), 3. layout, 4. colours (the layout's own, from the cover, from the logo). Fonts come with the layout; the editor has a folded "Fonts" list, each name in its own typeface. The style questions (words, inspiration images, apps they like, what to avoid, what first) are gone.
- The listing's full amenity list (`answers.listingAmenities`, up to 120) fills each room of the house guide with a line and a short text (guides made before 28 Oct 2026 do not have it).
- Reading the listing: no guessing. The function pulls the page's own data (overview, house rules section, amenities, sleeping arrangements, rating) and Claude only copies it; anything the page does not say stays empty. Only the tagline and welcome text are written by Claude (marked "Suggested by Claude").
- Optional section "Ratings and reviews" (off by default, one yes or no): the generator writes `content/reviews.json` {mode:"live", rating, count, source, url, quotes:[]}. Live, not curated: the guide shows the listing rating and latest reviews as they are. The engine does not show it yet (GUIDE builds that section).
- Listing data read from Airbnb's page data: the photo tour (rooms with their photos, so rooms are exact, not guessed), sleeping arrangements ("Bedroom 1: 1 king bed"), overview counts, house rules, amenities, rating. Since 12 Nov 2026 also the host's own words (`pageWords`): the whole description with its parts (The space, Guest access, Other things to note; the registration numbers left out), the rules the host added, and where the page says the house is (the location shown, and the town on its map when Airbnb gives it, which it does not always: without it the town comes from the house's position, GeoNames). Before that a read depended on the page reader for every sentence of the host's, and came back with no description, no town and no interests when the reader landed on another page; such an answer is now refused (`readerIsListing`: the reader's final address must be the listing site's, its title not an address). Only the reader sees the check-in and check-out times (the page a server gets from Airbnb never has them, with or without dates in its address). Since 13 Nov 2026: for an Airbnb room the reader is asked for the same room on Airbnb's British site, alone and afresh (`readerAsks`: asked for airbnb.com it landed on an advertising tracker every time, on airbnb.co.uk, .es and .fr the listing came back whole, with "Check in after 15:00"), Airbnb's own first page is refused as an answer, and the first stage ("page") no longer waits for the reader when the page itself was read with its photos (about 2 s instead of 8): the reader is asked while Claude writes (stage "write"), and the times its text states are copied into the answers Claude left empty (`timesOf`: only after the words that announce a time, never a rating or a date). Without stages, or when the page gave nothing, the reader's text joins the page's as before. /api/listing returns a `report` (how it was read, what was found) that the Load step shows as a found / not found list. Via the page reader only the visible text is available, so sleeping arrangements and exact rooms are usually missing there.
- When the writing assistant (Claude) fails, /api/listing still returns what the page states exactly (counts, times, rules, sleeping arrangements, rating, photos with rooms) and `report.assistant = 'failed'` with the technical `code`; the Load step shows an orange warning with that code. If nothing could be read either, it returns an error message.
